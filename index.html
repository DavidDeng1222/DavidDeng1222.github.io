A closer look
at human motion.

Motus is a local-first badminton motion laboratory. It brings source video, 2D body observations, selected-athlete 3D replay and racket-direction assistance into one inspectable workflow.

The engineering question is specific: how do you make replay continuous while keeping every observation tied to the correct person and source moment?

Continuous playback, recognition evidence and spatial presentation have separate responsibilities. A smoother replay must remain traceable to the source video.

2.3

Racket direction has an evidence hierarchy.

Visual racket regions are evaluated before they can support a direction. When only arm geometry is useful, a wrist/forearm aid can provide a clearly labelled presentation reference. That aid does not establish the racket head, racket-face angle or a measured 3D trajectory.

03 / Working method

A question.
An experiment.
A better question.

Product direction and engineering judgment become visible in the choices between iterations.

01

Define the observable.

Start with something the recording can support: a visible joint, a source timestamp or an inspectable racket region. Define the claim before evaluating the output.

02

Preserve the failure.

Keep false positives, gaps, person changes and rejected candidates. The source moment remains available after the algorithm changes.

03

Measure the right thing.

Test execution, display continuity and recognition accuracy answer different questions. Each result gets its own definition, scope and limit.

04

Close the product loop.

Connect the experiment to importing, replaying, inspecting and revising. A useful result should be reachable in the product, with its evidence intact.
